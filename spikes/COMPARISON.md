# Comparison: due-core and counterpart against criteria 1 and 2

Inputs: [due-outcomes/RESULTS.md](due-outcomes/RESULTS.md) (due commit
`8d56df2`, 208 rows), [counterpart-outcomes/RESULTS.md](counterpart-outcomes/RESULTS.md)
(counterpart commit `8fa4645`, 110 rows). Same machine (macOS 27.2.1, arm64),
same fake-provider behavior per variant, same survivor check. The table below is
generated by `spikes/make-comparison.py` from the two `results.jsonl` files.

Criteria, from the README: (1) every run ends in exactly one stated outcome,
never a hang or silence; (2) after any outcome the provider and its descendants
are gone, or the result says cleanup could not be confirmed and what remains.

## Measured: how each cell is decided

- **C1** is met when the caller received a stated outcome (completed, failed,
  timed out, interrupted). Otherwise the cell names the gap: `HANG` (no result
  at the harness limit: 12 s for rows with no deadline that are expected to
  hang, 30 s otherwise), `Err` (an error instead of a result), or `no result`
  (the future or handle was dropped, so none exists by construction; these rows
  are left out of the C1 count).
- **C2** is met when no fake-provider process was alive 5 s after the call
  returned (due one-shot) or 5 s after the worker was closed and the engine or
  supervisor shut down (due resident, counterpart). Otherwise the cell lists the
  survivors. `ok*` means something was still alive 5 s after the result and only
  the close or shutdown removed it (due's resident worker; any run that had not
  settled).
- The cell also shows the time to the result in ms. For due one-shot HANG rows
  the survivors are measured after the future is dropped at the limit.
- Rows run by all three sides: 88 (44 per provider; the `orphan` variants,
  `gd`, `gr` and due's streaming rows are excluded because not every side ran
  them). Counterpart was not run on `gd` and `gr` (no live permission request in
  its model). The `orphan` variants were run on counterpart only.

## Measured: differences that the table does not show

- **Cancel and timeout overhead.** Timeout 2000 ms (`c`, `plain`): counterpart
  returned at 2074 ms (Claude) and 2060 ms (Codex); due one-shot at 4004 ms
  both; due resident at 2005 ms (provider still alive). Interrupt at 1000 ms
  (`d`): counterpart 1057 ms and 1052 ms; due one-shot 3004 ms for both; due
  resident `interrupt()` returned about 10 ms after the interrupt. A provider
  that ignores SIGTERM and SIGINT (`stubborn`, `c`): counterpart 3572 ms (Claude) and
  3560 ms (Codex) (SIGKILL after its 1500 ms grace), due one-shot 4006 ms and 4005 ms, due resident
  Failed/Protocol at 8006 ms.
- **Normal completion.** `a`: due one-shot 27 ms and 28 ms, due resident 29 ms,
  counterpart 64 ms (Claude) and 130 ms (Codex).
- **Provider crash with a descendant holding stdout** (`e` `pipe`): due one-shot
  and due resident hang (30 s limit) for both providers; counterpart returns
  Failed at 568 ms and 631 ms.
- **Result after a completed run.** Counterpart leaves nothing alive after `a`;
  due resident leaves the provider alive until shutdown.
- **Dropping the owner.** Counterpart `j2` (handle dropped): all variants,
  including `setsid`, gone by 5 s. Due resident `j2` (engine dropped): Claude
  leaves `leaf-setsid`, Codex leaves `child`, `child-ignore`, `mid`,
  `leaf-setsid`. Due one-shot `j` (task aborted): all gone except `setsid`.
- **Descendants detached from their parent before any listing links them**
  (`orphan`): counterpart leaves `leaf-setsid` and reports
  `settled_within_observed_scope` with no warning. Not run on due, which tracks
  no descendants (its `setsid` rows already leave the leaf).
- **What the result says.** due: a status, an `ErrorKind` and a message; in
  failure paths messages such as "native shutdown deadline exceeded; settlement
  unconfirmed" with no process list. Counterpart: status, `error_code`, signals
  sent with timestamps, `cleanup_reason`, per-process `process_observations`
  with `cleanup.state`, `known_descendants_absent` and an `assurance_scope`
  string; no row produced a warning about an escaped descendant.

## What each side does that the other lacks

Measured, from the rows above:

- due one-shot and resident: a distinct `TimedOut` status (counterpart reports a
  timeout as `failed` with `error_code` `turn_timeout`); a faster normal
  completion; resident sessions that stay alive between assignments with live
  permission requests, a reject mode (`gr`: Failed/Unsupported in 33 ms to
  2.0 s) and an assignment timeout that ends an unanswered request (`gd`:
  TimedOut at about 3.0 s).
- counterpart: a default deadline on every turn (600000 ms, parsed; not waited
  for); descendant tracking that kills a `setsid` grandchild whose ancestry it
  has seen; cleanup that starts when the provider exits by itself, so a crash
  with children or a pipe holder ends in about 0.6 s; bounded escalation for
  providers and descendants that ignore signals; no process left after a
  completed run; cleanup on a dropped handle; a structured account of signals
  sent and of the scope of its cleanup claim.

Read from the source, not measured here: due has a versioned `RunResult`
schema, a CLI, an MCP server and JSON-RPC adapters; counterpart has pair
review, snapshots and recall.

## Gaps that neither side closes

1. **A descendant that detaches before it is observed.** Counterpart: `orphan`
   rows (6 of 6). Due: not run, but it has no tracking at all.
2. **Silence about what remains.** Neither result names surviving processes.
   Counterpart's `settled_within_observed_scope` appears on the `orphan` rows
   with a survivor, with `capture_gap_observed` false; the only caveat is a
   constant that also appears on clean rows.
3. **A refused outcome.** Neither has a status for it. due's nearest is
   `Failed/Unsupported`; counterpart has none, and runs no live permission
   requests.
4. **Hang detection depends on a deadline.** due has no default; counterpart's
   default is ten minutes, and the rows only show that it is parsed. Whether a
   ten minute hang satisfies "never a hang" is a requirements question.
5. **Codex crash timing.** Counterpart tracking misses the grandchild when the
   provider crashes within one capture interval of the grandchild's creation
   (Codex `e`, 5 of 8 and 4 of 8 repeats left the leaf).
6. **Real providers and Linux.** Neither run used a real CLI or Linux.

## Measured: where counterpart is worse than due

- Normal completion is slower: 64 ms and 130 ms against 27 ms and 28 ms.
- A timeout is `failed` with an error code, not its own status. A caller reading
  only `status` cannot tell a timeout from a provider failure.
- On the `orphan` rows its result asserts settlement within the observed scope
  without a warning, where due makes no claim at all.
- It models no live permission request, so cases `gd` and `gr` have no
  counterpart row.

## Judgment: revised recommendation for the delega core

This section is opinion drawn from the measurements above.

**Build the delega core on counterpart's runner and take due's contracts and
surfaces on top of it.** Reasons:

1. The criteria the README calls the core are the ones counterpart meets and
   due does not. On the 88 common rows counterpart met C2 in all 88 and C1 in
   62 of 76, with the only C1 misses being rows that wait out a ten minute
   default. due one-shot met C2 in 72 and due resident in 51 (after shutdown).
   The due failures come from the wrappers' process handling, which due-core
   does not own; counterpart's runner owns its own.
2. The cost is small and measured: 2 s on timeouts and cancels is replaced by
   about 50 to 140 ms, and a normal turn takes 64 to 130 ms instead of about
   28 ms.
3. The one gap counterpart leaves, descendants that detach before they are
   observed, is also left by due, and counterpart at least bounds it by
   tracking from spawn. It can be narrowed inside the runner without changing
   any contract.
4. due's remaining advantages are surfaces, not core behavior: a distinct
   timed-out status, resident sessions with live permission requests, a CLI,
   result schemas. Those can be layered over the runner. The resident engine
   should wait until it can meet criterion 2 after every outcome, because its
   worker outliving the run is by design what criterion 2 forbids.

**Changes the measurements point to, in either case.**

- Report a timeout as its own status, and add a refused outcome.
- Make the result say when a descendant could have escaped: a `capture_gap`
  that is true whenever the provider exited or a stop began before a capture
  covered its children, not only when a capture ran long. In the `orphan` rows
  it was false.
- Narrow the orphan gap in the runner. On Linux a subreaper or a cgroup would
  contain it; on macOS I know of no equivalent, and a first capture before the
  provider runs user code (for example by capturing immediately after each
  fork-exec) would reduce but not close it. This is a suggestion, untested.
- Decide whether a ten minute default turn timeout satisfies "never a hang", or
  whether a shorter default or an idle (no output) deadline is wanted, and
  measure the chosen value firing.

**What I am unsure of.**

- Everything depends on the fakes. Real tool processes put themselves in their
  own process groups, which makes the `setsid` and `orphan` variants the likelier
  ones in practice, and how often real providers create descendants that detach
  before a 500 ms capture is unknown.
- The real CLIs' behavior on stdin EOF, SIGINT and SIGTERM was not measured, and
  due's Codex rows depend on the fake not cleaning up its own children.
- Linux was not run. Counterpart's tracking uses `ps` start times, and due's
  wrappers use `PR_SET_PDEATHSIG` on Linux only.
- The 600000 ms default was read as a parsed value and never observed firing.
- The Codex `e` race was measured at one crash time only.
- Counterpart's long-lived features (pair review, snapshots) were not part of
  this comparison and I make no claim about them.
