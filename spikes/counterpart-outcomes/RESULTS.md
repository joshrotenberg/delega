# Results: does counterpart's runner meet criteria 1 and 2?

Same criteria and harness shape as [the due results](../due-outcomes/RESULTS.md).
Everything under "Measured" comes from `results.jsonl` (110 rows) and
`repeats.jsonl` (32 repeat runs) in this directory. The side-by-side with due is
in [../COMPARISON.md](../COMPARISON.md).

## Environment

- macOS 27.2.1 (build 26B131), arm64, `/bin/bash` 3.2.57, cargo 1.100.0.
- counterpart commit `8fa4645`, crate `counterpart-core` with feature
  `workers`, dependency versions from counterpart's `Cargo.lock`.
- 12 rows ran concurrently; differences under about 100 ms mean nothing.
- Every lifecycle bound at its default, read from the config at run time and
  recorded in each row's note: `kill_grace` 1500 ms, `result_grace` 1000 ms,
  `stop_deadline` 10000 ms, `drain_bound` 2000 ms, descendant tracking on.
  The capture interval is a constant of 500 ms in the source.
- What differs from the due run: counterpart starts one provider process per
  turn and has no resident worker; there are no permission-request cases `gd` and `gr`; `j` drops a `wait` future and `j2` drops the `SupervisorHandle`.

## How the counterpart results map to the README outcomes

The agent's `status` is `completed`, `failed` or `interrupted`. A timeout is
`failed` with `error` "Turn exceeded timeout" and `error_code`
`turn_timeout`; a caller interrupt is `interrupted` with `error_code`
`turn_interrupted`. There is no separate timed-out status and no refused status.

## Measured: summary counts

"Outcome stated" means the agent settled as completed, failed or interrupted
with a null pid. Rows `j` and `j2` end by dropping a future or the handle, so
no result is read by construction.

| claude | 55 | 37 | 7 | 11 | 39 | 47 of 50 |

The seven rows with no outcome at the 12 s harness limit are case `f` in six
variants and case `h`. Each carries counterpart's default turn timeout of
600000 ms (see below), so they would end at ten minutes; none was waited that
long. In all seven the later `stop(close)` and `shutdown()` left nothing alive.
The 3 (Claude) and 4 (Codex) rows still unclean after close and shutdown are the
`orphan` variants of `c`, `d`, `e` and, for Codex, `e` `orphan-late`.
`shutdown()` returned `Ok` in all 50 rows that reached it, per provider.

## Measured: what was asked for

1. **Default turn timeout.** Rows without an explicit timeout parsed
   `timeout_ms` as 600000. This is the value `worker_input::parse_spawn`
   returned, recorded in each row's note. I did not wait for it to fire. An
   explicit 2000 ms (`c`) and 3000 ms (`fd`, `hd`, `id`, `ed`) fired at the
   times in the table. Silent hang `f` and closed stdout `h` therefore did not
   return within the 12 s limit, and `stop(close)` ended them in 45 to 1565 ms.
2. **Grandchild in its own session.** Variant `setsid` (the child lives on and
   its grandchild leaves its session at once): tracked and killed in `c`, `d`
   and `fd` for both providers, and in `e` for Claude (8 of 8 repeats) with
   `descendants_after_exit`. For Codex `e` it was killed in 3 of 8 repeats
   (see below). `orphan-late` (child lives 1.3 s) behaves the same way.
3. **Grandchild that detaches before the first capture.** Variant `orphan`
   (the child exits immediately, so the grandchild is reparented to init
   before any listing links it to the provider): `leaf-setsid` survived in
   every `c`, `d` and `e` row, both providers, and was still alive 5 s later
   and after close and shutdown (harness killed it). The result said
   `settled_within_observed_scope`, `cleanup_reason` null, empty
   `cleanup_warnings` and `capture_gap_observed` false. The only hint in the
   result is the constant `unobserved_descendants_possible: true` and the
   `assurance_scope` text, which are also present on clean rows.
4. **Crash with a child holding stdout.** `e` `pipe`: Failed in 568 ms (Claude)
   and 631 ms (Codex) with `cleanup_reason` `process_group_after_exit`, no hang.
   `ed` `pipe` the same. Counterpart signals the group when the leader exits,
   so the pipe holder is killed. Crash with same-group children: all killed,
   both providers, in `e` `child`, `ignore` and `pipe`.
5. **Provider that ignores signals.** `c` `stubborn` 3572 ms for a 2 s
   timeout: SIGTERM at 2 s, SIGKILL 1.5 s later (`signals_sent`). `d`
   `stubborn` 2557 ms for an interrupt at 1 s. Children that ignore SIGTERM
   (`ignore`): the tracked descendants were signalled about every 57 ms (26 times for Claude and 25 for Codex in `c`),
   then SIGKILL; `c` `ignore` returned at 3593 ms and 3581 ms. All
   rows clean.
6. **When cleanup could not be confirmed.** No row produced a failed cleanup
   (every observation reported `settled_within_observed_scope`, 261 of 261)
   and none reached the 10 s `stop_deadline`, so the failure report path was
   not exercised. One observation carried a warning ("Pre-stop process capture
   exceeded 25 ms; newly detached children may be unobserved"), in the close of
   `codex-j-child`; that row was clean.
7. **Overhead on cancel and timeout.** `c` (2000 ms timeout) returned at
   2053 to 2137 ms for `plain`, `child`, `pipe`, `setsid`, `orphan-late`
   across both providers, 53 to 137 ms over the timeout, against due
   one-shot's 4004 ms (an unconditional 2 s grace). `d` (interrupt at 1000 ms)
   returned at 1051 to 1125 ms against due's 3004 ms. A normal completion costs
   more than due: `a` returned at 64 ms (Claude) and 130 ms (Codex), against
   27 ms and 28 ms for due one-shot buffered and 29 ms for due resident.
8. **Dropping the caller.** `j` (the `wait` future dropped at 1 s): the run
   continues, the provider and its descendants stay alive until `stop(close)`,
   which leaves nothing alive, `setsid` included. `j2` (the `SupervisorHandle`
   dropped at 1 s with no shutdown): `plain`, `child`, `pipe` and `setsid`
   were gone at 1 s after the drop and `ignore` was gone at 5 s (a zombie
   `provider` at 1 s), both providers.
9. **After a completed run.** `a` and every settled row: no process alive 1 s
   and 5 s after the result, both providers. Counterpart keeps no idle worker.

## Cases not exercised

- Real Claude Code and Codex, and Linux. Only macOS and bash fakes.
- The 10 minute default firing, and `stop_deadline` expiry (the cleanup
  failure report).
- The Bash permission guard (`allowed_tools` was empty, so no hook ran).
- Multiple turns on one worker, follow-up messages, queued messages.
- A provider that is slow to start (counterpart's version-probe timeout).
- Crash times other than 0.5 s, so the capture race is characterised at one
  point only.
- Output volume (counterpart's 2 MB line limit and event ring).
